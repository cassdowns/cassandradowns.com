In 2021 I dug a hole in my backyard. It was lockdown, I had nowhere else to go, and I'd noticed the soil wasn't very interested in growing vegetables - but maybe I could still make use of it.

I'd picked up the remnants of a home studio from a man whose wife had passed 15 years prior, who finally felt ready to let go, and inherited all the glaze ingredients, tools, materials, references, and supplies I could ever ask for - including a gas kiln and wheel - and set off trying to teach myself the art of pottery in the shed of my typical Melbourne post-war weatherboard rental. It was the kind of house that had been thrown up fast and cheap sometime in the fifties, on what used to be someone's cabbage patch a stone's throw from the old munitions works. Hence the inhospitable soil. My chickens routinely dug up chunks of lead and melted glass, which promptly discouraged our home grown veggie plan... but I did notice it had quite a high clay content.
